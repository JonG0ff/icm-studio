# Content Brief Format

Use this blank structure inside the workspace doing the work. It is a method, not a separate planning stage or tracking system.

```markdown
# Brief: [title]

- slug: [lowercase-hyphenated-slug]
- created: YYYY-MM-DD
- review: taste
- owner: [name or unknown]
- brand_or_project: [name or none]
- output_intent: [video | split-test-cell | copy-only]
- format: [selected workspace recipe]
- platform_and_placement: [destination]
- duration_or_size: [target]
- aspect_ratio: [video only]
- delivery: [files, captions, versions, deadline]
- approval: [pending, or exact prior approval and source]

## Request And Objective

Original request, intended outcome, and why it matters.

## Audience And Angle

Who it serves, what they know, one clear angle, and ordered key messages.

## Content Direction

Opening or hook, body structure, offer, CTA, and promised payoff.

## Visual And Audio Direction

Relevant mood, look, voice, music, typography, captions, and accessibility needs.

## Context And References

| Source | Path or URL | Selected sections | Use and rights limits |
|---|---|---|---|

## Constraints

Must include, must avoid, approved claims, length, platform, rights, tools, and budget limits.

## Success Criteria

How the result will be judged. For an experiment: hypothesis, test stage, primary metric, and read plan.

## Production Plan

Chosen format, stage sequence, required assets, missing inputs, owners, and next action.

## Open Questions And Decisions

Unknowns, blockers, exact accepted decisions with their source, and pending approvals.
```

Use the user's current request first. Read selected library pointers next. Ask only for material gaps; keep unknowns explicit. A legacy accepted spec can supply these fields without rewriting its decisions or requiring duplicate approval.
