# SOP: Footage-Led Short Quality

- approved_by: the repository owner. Re-approve or edit it for your own work.
- scope: footage-led short-form video; primarily Instagram/Reels unless the brief selects another destination

Load this SOP for similar footage edits. The producing workspace owns its brief, selections, edit map, review artifacts, and QA evidence. This record owns the approved reusable quality standard.

## Hook, Retention, And Payoff

- Default to a tight 9:16 social edit when no other format is requested. Confirm placement and safe zones in the brief.
- Give the viewer a concrete reason to stay in the first two seconds. Use source-supported stakes, useful advice, a clear problem, or a specific promised result.
- Cut recap introductions, throat-clearing, repetition, and dead time when they add no meaning. Preserve qualifications, context, and the speaker's intent.
- Make the sequence progress toward the promised payoff. Each hold, cut, caption, and graphic should help the viewer understand the point.
- Name a relevant interaction goal in the brief, such as applying or saving useful advice, sharing a relevant insight, or answering a meaningful question. Do not add a random CTA to satisfy a template.
- Treat these as editorial goals, not guarantees of reach, engagement, or algorithmic preference.

## Every Graphic Earns Its Space

- Use graphics to show the idea, clarify a relationship, or direct attention to the current point.
- Remove repeated speaker names, generic header straplines, footer explanations, section labels, progress rows, and ornamental cards when they only occupy space.
- Keep the speaker and captions prominent. Protect the face, meaningful gestures, readable text, and platform safe zones.
- Use purposeful entrances, readable holds, and completed exits. Avoid decorative motion that continues after it has conveyed its meaning.
- Keep authored hooks and explanatory graphics distinct from quotations and subtitles.
- Preserve required attribution. Choose on-screen, companion-file, or post-caption placement from the documented source terms and job's rights decision. Do not assume a credit can be moved or removed.

## Accurate Words And Highlight Bounds

- Verify subtitle words against the actual audio. Automatic transcription is a draft, including apparently high-confidence words.
- Check timing against speech. Do not claim word alignment from segment timings or a transcription file alone.
- Keep phrase breaks readable and leave enough time to read. Correct omissions, duplicated words, late captions, and clipped first or final words.
- A highlighted word's bounds must fit that word. Put interword spaces outside the highlighted span; do not include a leading gap in its colored box.
- Inspect real rendered frames at word changes and line wraps. Verify the active word, box placement, glyph fit, caption width, and face clearance.

## Mandatory Full Playback QA

Before presenting a rendered video for review or delivery, watch and listen to the complete final export. This is an audit in the existing workflow, not an additional human approval gate.

- Review the actual delivery file after final encoding and audio processing, from its first frame through its end.
- Check opening context, every cut, caption wording/timing, graphics, transitions, and the completed payoff.
- Catch unexpected frozen video, stalls, missing or repeated motion, audio gaps, audio/video drift, clipped syllables, and mismatched audio/video endings.
- Distinguish a deliberate hold or pause from an accidental stop. Review suspicious sections against the source and fix defects before presentation.
- Inspect at the intended mobile size and check muted comprehension where relevant.
- Record the exact filename/version, playback scope, findings, and fixes in the job's QA notes. If the final export changes, replay the changed result before presenting it.
- Use decode checks, duration/frame counts, loudness measurements, waveforms, and sampled frames as supporting evidence. They do not substitute for complete audiovisual playback.
- If complete playback cannot be performed, mark final-video QA `blocked`, state the limitation, and name the needed action. Do not mark the render ready for review or delivery. A packet-only review can still describe packet readiness while leaving final-video QA blocked.

Owner acceptance and publishing approval remain separate from production QA. Automatic stage continuation never waives these quality checks or creates a ship approval.
