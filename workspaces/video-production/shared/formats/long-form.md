# Long-Form Video Recipe

Use the shared stages from brief through delivery. A longer duration also needs structure, continuity, and a delivery plan.

## Brief And Script

- Specify platform, audience knowledge, runtime range, aspect ratio, publishing destination, and deliverable versions.
- Outline chapters with a question or purpose, promised takeaway, estimated duration, and transition per chapter.
- Script the opening promise, chapter progression, examples, recaps, and CTA. Mark interviews, demonstrations, and quoted material separately.
- Track claims and source notes. Preserve terminology and story logic across chapters.
- Estimate each chapter and total runtime. Avoid stretching a short-form hook sequence into a long video.

## Scenes And Assets

- Give scenes chapter and sequence IDs. Plan establishing shots, coverage, inserts, screen recordings, continuity, and transitions.
- Set pacing by comprehension and story purpose. Holds may be useful for demonstrations or interviews; no universal rapid-reset rule applies.
- Maintain a continuity list for people, location, wardrobe, props, screen state, grade, and audio ambience.
- Group the manifest by chapter/sequence. Include reuse, rights scope, source, duration, resolution, crop, and backup coverage.
- Agree a candidate target from coverage needs and available budget. Do not multiply short-form candidate counts across every minute.

## Edit And Delivery

- Record chapter timecodes, graphics conventions, music transitions, ambience, dialogue cleanup, and loudness targets agreed for the destination.
- Maintain versions and a change list across full-length, chapter, trailer, and other requested exports.
- Deliver the master, requested platform encodes, caption files, chapter metadata, thumbnail/cover notes, credits/attribution, and project handoff where requested.
- QA the whole video for story logic, pacing, chapter timing, continuity, audio consistency, captions, rights, and export settings. Spot checks alone do not establish full-video readiness.
